\section{Analysis}\label{sec:analysis}
\paragraph{What GRPO optimised.} Over training, the share of rollouts penalised by the sense judge fell from \x{penSenseFirstPct}\% (first 20 steps) to \x{penSenseLastPct}\% (last 20), and by the leak checker from \x{penLeakFirstPct}\% to \x{penLeakLastPct}\%, while mean$(p_B)-$mean$(p_C)$ on unpenalised rollouts stayed at \x{gapFirst} versus \x{gapLast} (Figure~\ref{fig:decomp}).
Mean training reward rose from \x{grpoFirstReward} to \x{grpoLastReward} and dev reward from \x{grpoDevFirst} (step \x{grpoDevFirstStep}) to \x{grpoDevLast}, almost entirely by avoiding the $-1$ penalties. Both $p_B$ (\x{grpoFirstPB} to \x{grpoLastPB}) and $p_C$ (\x{grpoFirstPC} to \x{grpoLastPC}) decreased.
With penalties this frequent, the cheapest way to raise reward is to become coherent and cautious, not to hint better; this is consistent with the unchanged insider advantage at test time.
\paragraph{The advantage lives in the helper slot.} Table~\ref{tab:slots} shows that after SFT, B identifies the helper far more often than C (\res{S1}{B_helper} vs.\ \res{S1}{C_helper}), but B and C identify the venue at similar rates (\res{S1}{B_venue} vs.\ \res{S1}{C_venue}).
This is consistent with venue hints acting mostly as public clues that the outsider can read without the memories: in the S1 and S3 training transcripts, among the strongest venue-predictive phrases in A's turns (smoothed log-odds; \path{results/public_clues.json}) are generic descriptions such as \emph{stalls} (night market), \emph{and green} (botanical garden) and \emph{lanes} or \emph{pins} (bowling alley), which the leak checker deliberately does not ban. Person hints, in contrast, have to point to specific shared incidents.
\paragraph{Held-out outsider.} Because the reported B and C are also the reward listeners, a lower C could in principle reflect the speaker exploiting that specific prompt. The held-out C$_\text{alt}$ gives similar accuracy for every condition (Table~\ref{tab:main}), which argues against this; B, however, has no held-out counterpart.
\paragraph{The B speaker sometimes names items.} The frozen B speaker does not know the secret but sees the memories, and despite its instruction and three re-samples, its turns still name an inventory item in \x{bNamesS0}\% / \x{bNamesS1}\% / \x{bNamesS3}\% of test conversations for S0 / S1 / S3 (the true helper or venue in \x{bNamesTrueS0}\% / \x{bNamesTrueS1}\% / \x{bNamesTrueS3}\%). These are guesses, often wrong, and are visible to C. For S0 they coincide with higher C accuracy (C both-correct \x{cBothNamedS0}\% when B names an item vs.\ \x{cBothNotNamedS0}\% otherwise); for S1 (\x{cBothNamedS1}\% vs.\ \x{cBothNotNamedS1}\%) and S3 (\x{cBothNamedS3}\% vs.\ \x{cBothNotNamedS3}\%) they do not raise C, so they do not explain the insider advantage. The reward penalises only A's leaks.
\paragraph{Showcase.} In test episode 91 (pre-registered showcase; helper Meera, venue rooftop cafe; cover topic chess), the shared memories include ``you lost a coin toss to Meera'' at the lakeside park and two sneezing incidents, one at the rooftop cafe and one at the community hall (a confusable pair). The S1 speaker wrote: \emph{``remember that time someone we know who always seems to drop coins unexpectedly beat me at a simple game of chance? \ldots there's this spot where I once sneezed so loud it disrupted an important moment''}, and later \emph{``that incident took place somewhere cozy and quiet''}. B assigned 0.98 to Meera and 0.99 to the rooftop cafe; C assigned 0.03 and 0.05 and guessed Ishita and the lakeside park. On the same episode the S3 speaker referred to the purple coin-toss bet and the sneeze but B chose Ravi and the lakeside park, the place of the coin-toss memory. Across the 20 showcase episodes, B recovered both slots while C recovered none in one episode for S1 (this one) and one for S3; all 20 are viewable in \texttt{viewer/replay.html}.
\paragraph{Bloopers (GRPO training rollouts).} \emph{A public clue.} At step 93 (helper Ishita, venue botanical garden) A said \emph{``maybe we can incorporate something cozy and green \ldots like a lush space with lots of plants''}; C put 1.00 on the botanical garden, as did B, so the venue hint carried no insider advantage. \emph{A correct hint penalised by the judge.} At step 97 (helper Kabir, venue night market) B put 0.94 on the helper and 0.75 on the venue while C put 0.00 and 0.17, but the sense judge scored the conversation 0.00, so the reward was $-1$. A's turns jump between a story about a cat, last weekend's football match and ``aloe vera at a noisy spot'', and the B speaker's last turn breaks the cover (``What's the venue for Tara's party?''). Cases like this illustrate how the $-1$ penalties dominate the learning signal.
