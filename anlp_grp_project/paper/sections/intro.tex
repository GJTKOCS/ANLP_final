\section{Introduction}
People routinely say things in public that only their friends understand. A reference to ``the ER saga'' means nothing to a stranger and everything to
the person who was in the waiting room with you. Clark and Schaefer showed that speakers shape what they say so that overhearers cannot follow it
while addressees can~\cite{clark1987concealing}, and Clark and Wilkes-Gibbs framed reference itself as a collaborative process grounded in shared history~\cite{clark1986referring}.
Language-model agents increasingly share context with each other (memory, tools, earlier turns), so the same phenomenon is a safety concern: a monitor that
reads only the visible messages may fail to understand a coordinated exchange~\cite{motwani2024secret,mathew2024hidden}.

We ask: \emph{can a small model be trained to speak privately in public, using shared history as the key?}
In our game (Section~\ref{sec:task}) A must convey a helper and a venue to B in a short, natural conversation about a cover topic, in the presence of C.
The shared history is a bank of eight memories generated for every episode \emph{before} the secret is drawn from the people and places it mentions,
so any hint that does not depend on the memories is equally available to C.

\paragraph{Contributions.} (i) A task and data generator for covert dialogue over fresh shared history, with information boundaries enforced in one module and tested.
(ii) A training pipeline: supervised warm start on filtered conversations, then multi-turn GRPO with a reward that contrasts a memory-aware and a memory-blind listener built from the same model, following Kerckhoffs's principle~\cite{kerckhoffs1883} (the outsider knows the rules and sees solved examples).
(iii) An evaluation in which only the speaker changes between conditions, with a held-out outsider prompt and a mismatched-memory control.
(iv) Findings, reported with bootstrap intervals: the warm start creates a measurable insider advantage that the untrained speaker lacks; GRPO mainly reduces penalties (incoherence, leaks) rather than improving the advantage; the advantage lives in the helper slot, while venue hints are largely readable by the outsider; and small trained listeners failed to learn the decoding task at our data scale. Section~\ref{sec:journey} documents the planned design and how the executed study departs from it.
