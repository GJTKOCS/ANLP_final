\section{From the Planned Design to the Executed Study}\label{sec:journey}
We report the design we set out to run, what did not work, and how we changed course. Every number in this section comes from the run logs.

\paragraph{The planned design.} The original plan used a 32B quantised generator served with vLLM for memories and warm-start conversations; \emph{trained} Qwen2.5-1.5B LoRA listeners for both B and C, with C retrained on the latest speaker's transcripts after every GRPO round so that the speaker would face an adapting outsider (an ``arms race''); two GRPO rounds of 150 steps; and five speaker conditions: S0 (base), S1 (SFT), S2 (best-of-8 sampling from S1, scored by the listeners), S3 (GRPO) and S4 (the large model prompted as speaker), plus a sweep over $\lambda\in\{0.5,1,2\}$. The data plan was 1500/200/300 train/dev/test episodes with four warm-start conversations per training episode. Five go/no-go checks (G1--G5) guarded the pipeline: memory quality (G1), warm-start headroom (G2), listener quality (G3), reward separation (G4) and GRPO health (G5).

\paragraph{Compute constraint.} All experiments ran on a single rented NVIDIA H100 (80\,GB) paid from a fixed credit allowance. The full plan above (two rounds, retrained listeners per round, five conditions and a $\lambda$ sweep) did not fit within that allowance once the warm start and listener diagnostics below had been run, so at each decision point we kept the components that the main question depends on and chose the next-best alternative for the rest. To fit the budget we used Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct in bf16 under plain HF generation instead of the 32B quantised model, \x{nTrain}/\x{nDev}/\x{nTest} episodes, three warm-start conversations per training episode, and one GRPO round of \x{grpoSteps} steps.

\paragraph{Failed attempt 1: the warm start leaked.} With the speaker prompt as originally specified, the 14B speaker named a person or place in 67\% of A turns; prompted B recovered both slots in 13.2\% of the 3000 conversations and prompted C in 10.3\%, so the outsider was almost as good as the insider (G2 requires B $\ge$ 50\%, C $\le$ 10\% and A leaks $\le$ 20\%; it failed). Re-sampling leaking A turns up to eight times (warm start only) cut the A leak rate to 5.8\%, but B fell to 9.5\% (C 4.6\%) and only 164 conversations qualified as good: without names, A's hints were vague and often pointed to the wrong memories. We then added to A's prompt the list of memories that mention the helper and the venue (information A already holds) with one example of the hinting style, and capitalised the option strings scored by the listeners. This gave B \x{warmBboth}\%, C \x{warmCboth}\%, an A leak rate of \x{warmLeak}\% and \x{warmGood} good conversations, which we used. G2 still did not meet its B bar of 50\% and was treated as a warning. The final prompt is identical for every speaker condition.

\paragraph{Failed attempt 2: trained listeners did not learn the task.} The 1.5B trained B listener reached \x{trainedLzeroB}\% dev both-correct at every epoch in two configurations, below the G3 bar of 10\% (already relaxed from the 30\% in the original plan). Diagnosis showed divergence to non-finite outputs and a collapse to a single prediction; after a fix (LayerNorm on pooled features, skipping non-finite updates) training losses decreased but dev accuracy remained at the level of guessing among the names in the memory bank (Appendix~\ref{app:trained}). A listener of this size and data scale could not provide a usable reward or evaluation.

\paragraph{The revised design.} We replaced both trained listeners with the same 14B model prompted zero-shot (Section~\ref{sec:method}), which separates insider from outsider (B \x{promptedDevB}\% vs.\ C \x{promptedDevC}\% both-correct on S3's dev transcripts). Because these listeners are both the reward and the evaluator, a lower outsider score could reflect the speaker exploiting that particular prompt; we therefore added a held-out outsider C$_\text{alt}$ that is never used in training. Since the listeners are now fixed, the arms race and the second GRPO round, which exist to let C adapt, no longer apply, and we ran one round. The best-of-8 baseline, the large-model speaker and the $\lambda$ sweep were not run within the remaining credit; we prioritised the conditions that isolate the two training steps (S0 $\to$ S1 $\to$ S3) and the held-out outsider check.

\paragraph{Go/no-go outcomes.} G1 failed (\x{slotFallbackPct}\% of memory slots fell back to a template), G2 and G3 failed as above, G4 passed (dev reward $-0.76$ for S0 vs.\ $0.56$ for good warm-start conversations), and G5, which is checked on the first 50 GRPO steps, failed (sense rate 0.65 over steps 41--50 against a bar of 0.8 and leak rate 0.052 against 0.05, although reward rose from $-0.42$ in the first third to $-0.29$ in the last). We continued past the failed checks and report their consequences as limitations rather than hiding them.
